# AIAA-BOBSAT-Hackathon-Fall-2026
This is the repository for the inaugural Hackathon Event hosted by our BOBSAT Team! It contains all of the information about the event, and will contain each submission and the judge feedback after the event is over.

# Event Overview
*"Bobcat Ballistics is developing a satellite constellation to blot out the sky for good. In support of this, they are searching for subcontractors to do all the work for them. As part of this, Bobcat Ballistics has released an RFP for a new EO satellite based on NASA's ongoing Landsat 10 RFP. They're is forking over *big contractor money* to student teams that present the best response to the RFP. These teams will go through a short period of time designing the satellite/mission using industry standard tools (Solidworks, STK, Simulink, MATLAB, Python, etc.) before presenting their work to a panel of Bobcat Ballistics auditors."*

This 1-week event is structured to be an intensive opportunity to get hands-on experience in Satellite Engineering. Teams will build off of the provided materials, with submissions graded based on (1) technical depth, (2) scope of implementation, and (3) clarity of both analysis and documentation. There will be members of the BOBSAT Team present in the meetings to give you guidance! Furthermore, during the weekend event a number of alumni will be available (virtually or in-person) who have space industry experience. They will be serving as judges during the final presentations. 

New Team Submissions will open September 14th, and all submissions/updates will close on September 19th at 12:30PM. Teams have the whole week to make contributions to their project, and teams are encouraged to continue researching and working outside of the in-person meetings. Everything you need will be hosted and managed entirely through this GitHub repository. The main Hackathon event will take place from 10am-4pm on September 19th in SSM 102. Final presentations will be 10 minutes long (or shorter depending on how many teams there are). Also, there will be free food! A full event schedule can be found in the Schedule file in this repository. 

Teams are expected to submit a number of deliverables throughout the event. These primarily consist of the project files used in your analysis and your final presentation. Teams should upload all of their files into a designated Box folder at the end of each weekday meeting. In order to be eligible for the participation prize of $20 per person, each team must meet the minimum requirements for the General Specification and *one* Detailed Specification. The remaining prize pool will be divided among the top 3 submissions. More detailed instructions and guides for the deliverables can be found in the rubric.

## General Specification: Required for All Teams
The general specification covers basic concepts that each team will need to demonstrate. As the team gets into the meat of the event (the detailed specifications), much of this preliminary work will be updated! These deliverables will primarily build up your communication skills, both verbal and visual. You'll also get some experience with making intelligible diagrams, a key skill for any engineer or scientist!

## Detailed Specification: Space Vehicle Engineering
The Space Vehicle Engineering specification covers the creation of model representations of the satellite, critical for performing the analyses necessary during the test phases of a project. This includes working with 3D CAD models in SOLIDWORKS and dynamics models in MATLAB Simulink. While this work is approachable to anyone with any background, students of ME, EE, and AE will find that these skills are particularly useful.  

## Detailed Specification: Mission Engineering
The Mission Engineering specification covers orbital analysis and concept of operations. These are extremely useful skills for people interested in working on satellite mission design. These deliverables involve working with Ansys STK, which is a cornerstone software used all throughout the space industry. The basics of STK are very reasonable to learn with any background, but AE and PHYS majors are aligned most with this work.  

## Detailed Specification: Simulations
This specification gives a decent tour of simulations that form the foundation of modern space vehicle design. Namely, the deliverables involve piecing together results from a number of code-based tools, including the Basilisk Orbital Simulation library, the F Prime embedded software framework, and the OpenCV image processing library. Becoming familiar with these domain-specific libraries is increasingly relevant for entry-level satellite engineers! While CSE, DS, and MATH majors will be most familiar with these tools, we encourage people out of these majors to give this a shot!

# Submitting Your Work
Each team's submission must follow a specific format for ease of collaboration and judging. 

## Submitting Deliverables
After forming your team, the event organizers will give you a link to a box folder where you can upload all of your deliverables. In this box folder, you should have:
1. A text document called Contributions. This should have sections for each member of the team that details what work they contributed to the submissions. It's ok if people work collaboratively on most of the project. However, each member should have things that they alone were responsible for. Otherwise they're freeloading $20...
2. A powerpoint called Presentation. This should contain your final presentation. Specific details on what should be within this presentation can be found in the "Guidance on Presentation" file in the General Specification folder of this repository.
3. A folder called Analyses. This should contain every deliverable called out in whichever Detailed Specification category you choose. If you made or modified something and it isn't in this folder, it won't count! Try not to clutter this folder with too much extra stuff.
4. (Optional) A folder called Resources. This can include files from the repository, links to useful resources, or anything else that you find in aid of your analyses.

It is a good idea to upload what you've done to the box frequently! There is very little time to grade everything that you submit, so you increase your chances of being graded favorably if the judges can do so on Wednesday rather than Saturday. With that in mind, you should upload everything you have at the end of each weekday meeting, even if it's not complete!!! 

## Creating Your Team
During the first weekday meeting, the event organizers will give you an overview of each of the specifications. Then, you'll be encouraged to form teams to focus on a single detailed specification. These specs are aligned with a suite of tools that you'll be learning during the event. Teams should probably be limited to 3-5 people, though this may change depending on event turnout. While the teams loosely align with some majors better than others, anybody can learn any of these processes! The team rosters will be captured maintained by the event organizers throughout, so update them if your team changes!

## Collaboration
At the end of each meeting day, the event organizers will ask one team member to upload the files you've been working on to your box folder. This is so that you can easily share the folder with your team members *and* so that the judges can grade your work before Saturday. Don't steal other people's work!
