As part of your presentation, Bobcat Ballistics requires that the analysts make use of block diagrams to convey aspects of the satellite

---

For this deliverable, the analysts should include at least one block diagram in their final presentation. Examples of what *could be* conveyed via block diagram include:
1. Power flows and electrical connections between components to accompany the power budget or wiring diagram
2. Information flow within the satellite and to the ground station to accommodate the orbital simulation or link budget
3. Heat sources and sinks, with data from a thermal simulation
4. Software architecture necessary for executing the satellite's basic functionality
5. Signal and Image processing steps to accompany a processing budget

---

For further consideration, the analysts could include:
1. A Functional Architecture to accommodate the Physical Architecture
2. Side-by-side comparisons of block diagrams for systems in a trade-off study

---

Bobcat Ballistics is providing the following resources for the analysts' consideration:
For reference, a [Block Diagram]([url](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Block_diagram)) is a graphic that displays elements of a system and the relationships between them as blocks and arrows respectively. For instance, the European Space Agency has an example of a rudimentary block diagram for one subsystem of a satellite [here]([url](https://gssc.esa.int/navipedia/index.php/System_Design_Details)). Block diagrams tend to be more efficient at conveying a system's structure than text. A capable tool for making these diagrams can be found [here]([url](https://app.diagrams.net/)).
