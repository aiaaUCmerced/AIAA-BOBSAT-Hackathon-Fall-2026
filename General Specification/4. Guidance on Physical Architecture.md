Bobcat Ballistics requires that the analysts assemble a rudimentary physical architecture for the satellite. That is to say, a list of physical components necessary for the satellite's operation. 

---

For this deliverable, the analysts should evaluate the handful of component options in the Physical Architecture Example Components spreadsheet and demonstrate/justify their choices. It should be noted that the components presented here are only a preliminary list! The analysts are encouraged to find and implement other options:
1. Payload System (Imager and/or Data Processing)
2. Electrical Power System (Generation, Storage, and Distribution)
3. ADCS (Attitude Determination and Attitude Control)
4. Structural System (Frame, Rails, and/or Heat Management)
5. C&DH System (Command Module)
6. Communications System (Radio Transmitter and Receiver)
These subsystems compose the satellite system. The Physical Architecture diagram should clearly show the relationship between the satellite system, its component subsystems, and each of their substituent elements.

---

For further consideration, the analysts could include:
1. More detail in the relationships between elements, such as including physical or electrical connections
2. A trade-off study comparing multiple different choices for components, with the contrast being showed by two different physical architectures
3. A Physical-Functional Allocation chart, showing which components in the satellite (Physical Architecture) correspond to which functions the satellite performs (Functional Architecture).

---

Bobcat Ballistics is providing the following resources for the analysts' consideration:

The Systems Engineering Book of Knowledge [article](https://sebokwiki.org/wiki/Physical_Architecture) on Physical Architecture. This gives background on the physical architecture and some example diagrams.

[Functional Architecture](https://sebokwiki.org/wiki/Functional_Architecture). The same, but for the functional architecture
