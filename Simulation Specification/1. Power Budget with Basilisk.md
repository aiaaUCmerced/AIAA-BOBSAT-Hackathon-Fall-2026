Bobcat Ballistics requires the contracted team to simulate the power drain and generation of a satellite in a day's period. 

---

For this deliverable, the analysts should set up and modify [this tutorial](https://avslab.github.io/basilisk/examples/scenarioPowerDemo.html). Specifically, you shall do the following:
1. Set up the Basilisk simulation library on your computer or on a Google Colab instance. Use the source code for the above tutorial (scroll down to scenarioPowerDemo.run and click source) and run it. The resulting power plot and Vizard simulation file should be saved to your team's Box folder.
2. Modify the simulation code to take place over a 24-hour period. Does the satellite generate enough electricity when it's tumbling like this? Add another solar panel to the opposite side of the satellite and generate a new plot + Vizard simulation. What about now?
3. Consult the specs for the physical architecture your team has assembled to modify the specs and location of your satellite. You can also incorporate one or more values for the power drain based on your spec sheets. Does your satellite have enough area to permit static solar panels (mounted to the sides of the satellite only)?

---

For further consideration, the analysts could implement the following:
1. Change the orbital parameters and initial conditions to match a Sun-Synchronous Orbit without tumbling. How does this change the power outlook?
2. Implement the changes seen in the [ADCS-integrated power demo](https://avslab.github.io/basilisk/examples/scenarioAttitudeFeedbackRWPower.html). How does this change your power budget outlook for a 2-hour period? A 24-hour period? Make the tumbling more extreme and demonstrate the magnitude of the results. The Vizard simulation will be illuminating here. 
3. Consider [this demo](https://avslab.github.io/basilisk/examples/scenarioSensorThermal.html), which lets you track the temperature of components. Consult the Basilisk examples in their documentation the technical specs for each of your components (mostly the battery) to refine your power demo to include power draw from heaters over a 24-hour window. How much does this change the variability of your power plot? Does it change the power outlook?
