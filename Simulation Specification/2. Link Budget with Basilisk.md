Bobcat Ballistics requires the contracted team to demonstrate the satellite's downlink capabilities through simulation. 

---

For this deliverable, the analysts shall deliver the following:
1. Consider the following [example](https://hanspeterschaub.info/basilisk/examples/scenarioGroundDownlink.html) (source code visible [here](https://github.com/AVSLab/basilisk/blob/develop/examples/scenarioGroundDownlink.py)). Configure the example correctly and generate the same plots from the example. Give an explanation of what each plot is communicating.
2. Modify the ground station so that it's centered at UC Merced. Modify the orbit to a Sun-Synchronous Orbit. How do the resulting plots change? It may be illuminating to expand the simulation window to 7 24-hour periods or to take a sample of multiple 24-hour periods starting on different days.
3. Modify your code with the changes from the [Ground Location Imaging example](https://hanspeterschaub.info/basilisk/examples/scenarioGroundLocationImaging.html) to include 5 national parks as target locations. Explain the result of each of the generated plots. 

---

For further consideration, the analysts could include:
1. A trade study examining the differences between a 500km, 600km, and 700km orbit. How much more data can the satellite downlink? Does increasing the altitude make the decrease in resolution worth it?
2. Add multiple ground stations and see how your results change. First consider ground stations only in California, then expand to cities across the globe.
3. Consider this [deorbit demo](https://hanspeterschaub.info/basilisk/examples/scenarioDragDeorbit.html). Play around with parameters that you can find online or derive based on spec sheets. If we wanted a satellite in orbit for 5 years, what are some combinations of parameters that would permit this?
